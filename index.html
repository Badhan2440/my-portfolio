About · Engineering as a way of thinking

My background is Computer Engineering, the layer where software meets the machine it actually runs on.

That training left me with a bias I have never shaken: understand the system end to end before touching a line of code.

In practice that means I am as comfortable profiling a hot loop as I am arguing about spacing on a landing page. I like problems where the constraint is real — latency budgets, memory ceilings, data that will not fit in RAM — because those are the ones where good engineering is visible in the result.

Right now I am focused on turning that depth into high-impact products: systems that hold up under load, and interfaces that make the complexity underneath feel like it was never there.
